06 / TRANSPARENT METHODOLOGY

Methodology and definitions

Calculation sequence

  1. Historical EBITDA: net income + interest expense + income tax expense + D&A.
  2. Seller presentation: historical EBITDA + all signed seller-origin claims, regardless of buyer treatment. May include pro forma claims.
  3. Buyer-assumed normalized earnings: historical EBITDA + assumed historical adjustments − linked owner replacement costs.
  4. Buyer operating case: normalized earnings + assumed pro forma adjustments − additional recurring operating costs.
  5. Cash before financing: operating case − maintenance capex − growth capex − incremental operating working capital − cash taxes − other cash requirements.
  6. Illustrative coverage: cash before financing / annual principal and interest. Zero debt service produces no coverage ratio.

Input conventions

Expense reconstruction inputs use the amounts already deducted from net income. Owner distributions are not income-statement expenses. Pass-through owner taxes are not automatically company income-tax expense. SDE must be reconciled to this EBITDA basis before entry.

All adjustments use the selected period. Historical normalizations, owner adjustments, and forward-looking pro forma changes remain distinct. Evidence references document sources; the tool does not authenticate them.

Working-capital input is an incremental operating cash requirement over the modeled year, excluding cash and financing debt. It is separate from the normal working-capital balance delivered at closing. Negative working-capital change represents a cash release.

Interpretation limits

No universal deal score, valuation recommendation, or automatic adjustment approval is generated. Missing cash inputs suppress the cash estimate. A clean issue list is not assurance about customer concentration, legal exposure, licensing, key-person dependency, or other unmodeled risks.

Reported multiples use your EV assumption. Small positive earnings can produce extreme multiples; values are not capped. The tool does not value earnouts, calculate tax step-ups, size loans, or forecast future cash flows.
